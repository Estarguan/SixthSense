# Ethics and responsible use

A system that listens and watches inside a home carries real responsibility.
These are the questions this project takes seriously and the commitments that
follow from them.

## Consent

- **People who live there** should know the system is on and what it does.
- **Guests and visitors** are monitored too. Tell them, or switch the system
  off when they are over.
- **Recording and wiretap laws differ by place.** Audio capture in particular is
  regulated in many jurisdictions. Check local law before deploying anywhere
  other than your own home.

## Misuse and coercive control

A tool that listens for conflict could be turned into a tool for controlling a
partner, a family member or a flatmate.

- Designed for safety alerts, not for tracking what a person says or does.
- Normal mode keeps no transcripts and no history of conversations.
- Anyone monitored should be able to know it is happening.
- Do not use it to watch an adult who has not agreed to it.

## Children and dependants

- Monitoring a child is different from monitoring an adult. Be proportionate and
  age appropriate, and tell older children it is running.
- Caregivers monitoring a parent should involve that parent in the decision.

## Accuracy and harm

- **False alarms cost something.** A wrongly alerted contact can panic, and a
  wrongly flagged argument can cause friction.
- **Missed events cost more.** The system can miss real emergencies. It must
  never be sold or described as a replacement for emergency services, a smoke
  alarm or a medical alert device. See
  [cases/false-positives-and-negatives.md](cases/false-positives-and-negatives.md).
- **Language and cultural bias.** Keywords and speech recognition are English
  only. Accents, dialects and loud but ordinary ways of speaking in some
  households may be misread.

## Third parties

Clips and generated text go to Google, ElevenLabs and Expo when triggered. Users
should be told this plainly. See [privacy.md](privacy.md).

## Commitments

1. Stay local by default, and escalate only when needed.
2. Never store footage or transcripts.
3. Say plainly what is sent, when and to whom.
4. Keep a human in the loop: the system alerts, it does not act on its own.
5. Document limitations as clearly as features.
